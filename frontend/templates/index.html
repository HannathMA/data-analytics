Temporal Behavioral Signatures via 24-Lag Pearson Autocorrelation

Standard K-Means on raw 168-hour smart meter readings fails because Euclidean spatial distance ignores temporal sequence and diurnal cyclicity. By transforming raw consumption into 24 hourly Autocorrelation (ACF) lags and thresholding non-significant values with a 95% Confidence Interval (|r| ≤ 1.96 / √N = 0.1512), we isolate domestic behavioral routines (such as evening peak recurrence, dual-shift commuting, and baseline flat refrigeration) while compressing dimensionality by 85.1%.

ACF Pearson Formulation & Null-Hypothesis Cutoff:
r_k = Pearson(X_{1..168-k}, Y_{1+k..168})
If |r_k| ≤ 1.96 / √168 ≈ 0.1512 → r_k = 0.0 (Noise Suppression)

Empirical Takeaways
Chart 1: Raw 168h Series

Raw Euclidean Distance Fails

Standard K-Means on raw 168-hour series suffers from the curse of dimensionality and phase sensitivity. Two identical domestic routines shifted by 60 minutes yield large Euclidean separation, while magnitude scaling obscures temporal habits.

Takeaway: Raw time series requires feature transformation.
Chart 2: 24-Lag ACF & 95% CI

Noise Rejection & 85.1% Compression

By computing 24 hourly Pearson autocorrelation lags and zeroing non-significant values outside the 95% confidence corridor (±1.96 / √N = ±0.1512), appliance stochastic noise is stripped, compressing 168 raw dimensions into a resilient 24-element signature.

Threshold: |r| > 0.1512 isolates true lifestyle cyclicity.
Chart 3: Centroid ACF Profiles

Identifiable Behavioral Archetypes

Centroids reveal stark domestic archetypes: strict daily evening routines (Lag 24 up to 0.98), dual-shift commuting routines (Lags 12 & 24), continuous thermal inertia loads (Lags 1–3 persistence), and aperiodic baseline refrigeration.

Action: Enables persona-targeted flexible EV tariffs.
Chart 4: Population Distribution

Grid Peak Ramp Concentration

The distribution reveals the proportion of domestic consumers belonging to high-ramp clusters versus flat baseloads. Utilities can calculate exact substation capacity reserve requirements by weighting peak-correlated clusters.

Application: Local distribution transformer risk modeling.
Chart 5: Silhouette Optimization

Optimal k via Silhouette Score Maximization

The cluster count is selected by maximizing the mean Silhouette Coefficient across k ∈ [2, 15] rather than heuristic elbow guessing. The Silhouette score peaks at k = 12 (s ≈ 0.578), corroborated by a global minimum in Davies-Bouldin Index (DBI ≈ 0.897).

Optimal Partition: Selected k maximizes mean Silhouette score and minimizes DBI.

94.95% Explained by 2 Latent Dimensions
